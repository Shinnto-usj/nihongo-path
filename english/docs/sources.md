# English Studio: content rationale and sources

Research checked on 2026-10-24. All practice scenes, question sets, speaking models, and benchmark prompts in `content/studio.js` are original. Their people, workplaces, results, and events are fictional or generic. They do not describe a learner or a real research project.

## What the target means

The Council of Europe explicitly distinguishes C2 from an idealised native or near-native speaker. Its descriptors offer useful language for advanced precision, appropriate register, fluency, interaction, and intelligibility; they do not by themselves diagnose an individual or certify a level. The Companion Volume discusses proficiency profiles across different activities, and treats intelligibility and effective prosody as important pronunciation goals, including where another-language accent remains.

Relevant locations in the 2020 Companion Volume are printed pages 37–41 (levels, profiles, and limits of descriptor scales), 48–49 (oral comprehension), 72–75 (interaction and discussion), and 133–134 (phonological control). Page references are printed page numbers, not a PDF viewer’s zero-based indices.

Source: [Council of Europe, CEFR Companion Volume (2020)](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4). Official publication entry: [CEFR Companion Volume and language versions](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions).

**Design inference:** a single overall examination score does not identify a particular learner’s current speaking, writing, pragmatic, or listening weaknesses. This app therefore starts with specific recordings and comprehension tasks. It does not infer a universal “native percentage”, map an old total score to an individual skill profile, or present a practice result as a CEFR diagnosis.

## Two different kinds of listening

**Built-in practice:** twelve fictional scripts of 100–170 spoken words, each with three original comprehension questions. Their purpose is controlled attention to implied stance, detail, conditions, scope, and discourse structure. Device text-to-speech is synthesis. It may flatten irony, hesitation, turn-taking, rhythm, and regional variation. Correct answers must be supported by the script; the exercises must not assume that synthetic intonation reliably conveys a hidden emotion.

**Publisher recordings:** linked manually from the resource page. No third-party transcript, audio file, embedded player, or tracking script is copied into the application. The listed pages provide published recordings or reference material. Link verification establishes that the publisher page describes the relevant resource; it is not proof that media playback will work in every browser, country, or network.

The learning instructions are this project’s suggested workflow, not a publisher guarantee that these activities produce a particular proficiency level. No study-hour promise is inferred from the sources.

## Speaking and comparison

The twelve output tasks cover explanation, disagreement, small talk, narrative, oral repair, requests, a short informal email, feedback, handling unknowns, register switching, synthesis, and a clear refusal. Their model responses are one possible solution. A different response can be effective; copying a model fluently is not the same as producing an appropriate response independently.

Six stable benchmark prompts support self-comparison. Keep recording conditions, preparation time, topic, and assistance similar; compare clarity, precision, relevance, interactional choices and the effort required to follow the message. Repeated familiarity can improve the performance on that prompt. Pair repeated benchmarks with a previously unused task to look for transfer, and use feedback from a suitable human interlocutor where possible. A self-checklist is neither independent assessment nor an automatic fluency, pronunciation, or “native” score.

## Source and verification boundaries

- Resources are identified by official publisher URLs and have no affiliate query or private parameters.
- External recordings are not rehosted. The app’s synthesized demonstration speech is not represented as publisher audio.
- The test suite checks content structure, word counts, IDs, answer bounds, model completeness, resource URL origins and the absence of score-certification fields. It does not psychometrically validate difficulty or replace human language review.
- Several Stanford podcast pages appeared in search, but direct retrieval did not succeed during this check. They were not added to the verified resource list.
